PointView

Rebecca Kong·Summer 2025·3 min read

PointView makes tennis self-analysis faster and cheaper. It is a website that automatically trims match recordings down to the points actually played, gives point statistics, and lets players take notes next to their video. The project was built over the summer in the Entrepreneurship Studio Program at Harvey Mudd College with teammates Emily Ing and Bell Zhou, advised by Coach Caitlin Bernard and Professor Zach Dodds.

Under the hood it combines a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model for players, GridTrackNet for the ball, an auto-trimming algorithm that decides when a point starts and ends, and a homography that maps what the camera sees onto a top-down “minicourt.”

The Website

Users upload a match with a title, match type and notes, then browse their matches sorted by date, type and title. Each match page shows the video next to a rich-text notes editor.

Player and Ball Detection

Players

Players are detected with a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model and given IDs, so only the people playing the current match are tracked and not line judges or ball kids.

The Ball

The ball is tracked with GridTrackNet.

Auto-Trimming

A rule-based algorithm watches the players’ positions, the serving position and the ball. A point starts when the players are in position and ends when the ball or players are missing for 50 consecutive frames, so downtime between points is cut. In one example a 2 minute 22 second recording of a game became 1 minute 12 seconds of play.

Mapping to a Birds-Eye View

The user labels keypoints on the court in the video. Those are matched to preset points on a minicourt to compute a homography matrix, which is then applied to the player and ball detections to place them on the minicourt. The ball’s path is interpolated between bounces.

My Contributions

  • Training: trained the customized YOLOv8 object-detection model for tennis footage, covering manual data collection and labeling, dataset preparation, training and evaluation.
  • Mapping: implemented the keypoint-to-minicourt mapping that combines player detection, ball tracking and coordinate mapping to turn raw match footage into a birds-eye view.

The Entrepreneurship Process

The Entrepreneurship Studio Program treated the summer as a startup: build something real, then keep pitching it. Over the weeks the team pitched PointView to a series of visitors and mentors, including an intellectual-property attorney, and showed it at the campus-wide demo day. Along the way the team also explored a subscription model with tiered plans.

More about how PointView was created and what I learned is coming soon.

Conclusion and Future Work

PointView runs reasonably accurately on high-quality footage. GridTrackNet’s ball detection depends on footage quality, which limits how well PointView can track the ball and generate stats from everyday recordings. Future work is a better algorithm for interpolating the ball path, or a higher baseline camera quality, while keeping PointView accessible.
