Mobile Lectern

Rebecca Kong·E4 team project·3 min read

The client is a Harvey Mudd College professor who prefers to stay close to his students. The classrooms he lectures in have no way to display notes at tables near students, and products he tried did not work, so he asked two E4 teams for help. He needed a way to reference his materials while standing, without a barrier between him and the class.

The result is a foldable, 3D-printed lectern that sets up in about three seconds, weighs 1.15 pounds and costs around $15 to make.

The Prototype Question

The final prototype was built to answer one question: does it consistently take less than five seconds to set up the lectern? That cannot be answered without a full prototype, and it decides whether the objective of minimizing set-up time is met. The same prototype also tested durability, portability and weight.

How It Works

  • Resists vertical forces: supports lock into place between the layers to counteract torque from added devices.
  • Adjusts height and angle: the supports between layers lock into three positions each, giving nine combinations for the top surface.
  • Resists lateral forces: the large bottom surface grips the table so the lectern does not slide while writing. Rubber feet could add more grip in the future.
  • Holds materials: a lip at the bottom of the angled top surface keeps notes, tablets and laptops in place.

Materials

The body is 3D-printed PLA, which is light, strong, cheap, available in many colors and printable on campus, so other professors could reproduce it. The hinges, screws and lock nuts are stainless steel, and locking nuts keep the hardware from loosening over time. Wood, bamboo and carbon fiber were considered but were harder to source and shape or could not balance weight and durability.

Testing

Ten participants each set up the collapsed prototype three times with no demonstration, timed from the first touch until it was set up properly. The average was 4.4 seconds on the first attempt, 3.4 on the second and 3.3 on the third. The third attempt matters most, since the client will use it several times a week, and it beats the five-second requirement.

Objectives and Constraints

ObjectiveResult
Minimize set-up time3.34 s average
Maximize durability4/5, held a laptop comfortably
Maximize portability3/5, 10 × 12 × 3 inches or less
Minimize weight4/5, 1.15 pounds

All client constraints were met: set up in under five seconds, under 3 pounds, a single piece, able to support unbalanced weight, manufacturable at HMC, and under $50.

Moving Forward

The hinges were slightly angled and grind when they rotate, because screwing into the PLA made alignment difficult. Future versions would improve hinge alignment and try lubricants or different hinge types, which would make the design more durable and environmentally friendly.
